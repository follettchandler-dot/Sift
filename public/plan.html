01 · Executive Summary

The pipe for itemized purchase data

Itemized is building the data infrastructure layer for itemized purchase intelligence. We’re not another personal finance app or expense tracker. We’re the pipe that makes item-level purchase data accessible to every consumer fintech, expense tool, and bank that needs it.

Plaid built the pipe for bank account data. Itemized is building the pipe for what you actually bought.

Today, $10.5 trillion in US consumer spending is locked at the merchant level. Every bank, Mint, Monarch, Expensify, Ramp — they all show the same thing: “Walmart $152.39.” The data exists at the item level inside POS systems, card networks, and merchant databases. Nobody has built the infrastructure to unlock it and make it accessible.

Itemized is that infrastructure. We aggregate item-level purchase data from email receipts, retailer APIs, POS partnerships, card networks, and (eventually) our own card. Then we expose it through an API that any fintech, bank, or business can integrate.

02

Item-level employee expense intelligence sold directly to mid-market companies. $25-50/seat/month. Highest-margin near-term revenue. Validates the value of item-level data and funds infrastructure development while we build the API.

03

Itemized’s own consumer app demonstrates the data product, builds the user base that powers the data engine, and serves users who want item-level intelligence directly. The consumer app is also the AI training engine — every receipt and correction makes the entire platform smarter.

The endgame

The default data layer

Itemized becomes the default data layer for any product that needs to know what was purchased, not just where. Like Plaid for bank data or Twilio for SMS, we become unkillable infrastructure that every fintech depends on.

03 · The Problem

$10.5 trillion is invisible

Consumers and businesses have no visibility into what they actually buy. Banks show them “Walmart $152.” They can’t see that $82 was groceries, $41 was office supplies, and $29 was toys.

For consumers

They know they spent $800 at grocery stores last month, but not that $200 of it was snacks, $150 was alcohol, and $100 was household supplies that should come from a different budget. Every personal finance app on the market shows merchant-level data because that’s all bank APIs return.

For businesses

A $14,000 quarterly gas station tab on company cards could be 70% fuel and 30% snacks and energy drinks. No expense tool breaks this down. CFOs are flying blind on what their employees actually buy.

For data buyers

CPG brands, retailers, market research firms, and hedge funds would pay millions for anonymized item-level purchase data across merchants. This data doesn’t exist at scale today. Whoever builds it first becomes a category-defining alternative data company.

04 · The Solution

Three pillars of infrastructure

Itemized is built on three layers that work together: ingest the data, process it with AI, expose it through APIs and products. Each layer is defensible. Together they’re unkillable.

01

Ingest — Data Sources

The hardest and most defensible part. We aggregate item-level data from every possible source: email receipt parsing, retailer API connections, photo OCR, POS partnerships, bank/card network Level 3 data, and eventually our own card. Every new source increases coverage and makes the API more valuable.

02

Process — AI Intelligence Engine

The AI layer that extracts items from raw data and categorizes them with 94% accuracy across 147 categories. Cross-merchant taxonomy means the same “snacks” category whether it’s from Walmart, Target, or a gas station. User corrections train both per-user AND cross-user models, creating a compounding flywheel.

03

Expose — Developer API

REST API, webhooks, and SDKs that let any developer integrate item-level purchase data in 5 minutes. Pay-per-receipt pricing ($0.02-0.10). This is the layer that turns Itemized into actual infrastructure that other fintechs build on top of.

Why nobody else has done it

Banks won’t. They make money on interchange fees. Showing customers exactly what they buy encourages smarter spending, which reduces bank revenue.

Card networks won’t. Visa and Mastercard are B2B infrastructure for banks and merchants. Building consumer products is outside their DNA.

Expense tools won’t. They’re focused on corporate expense management. They don’t have the AI engine or consumer products.

POS companies won’t. They serve merchants, not consumers. They have the data but no consumer distribution.

Apple/Google won’t. They only see their own wallet transactions. Single-channel by design. They’ll never aggregate across competing wallets and bank cards.

09 · Defensibility

Four compounding moats

Each moat compounds over time. Each one makes the next harder to replicate. Together they create insurmountable switching costs once we’re embedded in 50+ fintechs.

01

Multi-source data aggregation

No competitor has built the full ingestion stack across email, retailer APIs, OCR, POS, and card networks. Each new source compounds the moat. Each integration takes months for a competitor to replicate.

02

AI training data flywheel

Every receipt processed makes the AI smarter. Every user correction trains both per-user AND cross-user models. The longer we operate, the more accurate we become — and competitors can’t catch up because they don’t have the data.

03

API integration switching cost

Once 50+ fintechs depend on Itemized, switching means rewriting their product. This is Plaid’s moat. Stripe’s moat. Once we’re infrastructure, we’re permanent. Customer acquisition cost approaches zero as we become the default.

04

Aggregated data asset

The anonymized dataset becomes exponentially more valuable with scale. Hedge funds and CPG brands will pay seven figures for this data. This is the moat that makes us a category-defining alternative data company — and an irresistible acquisition target.

14 · Risks & Mitigations

What could go wrong

Big tech (Plaid, Stripe) builds competing product

First mover advantage. Focus on AI categorization moat. Become acquisition target before they catch up.

POS companies won’t partner

Software layer alone covers 85-90%. Direct enterprise revenue funds growth without partnerships. Multiple POS targets to pursue.

API adoption slow

Start with design partners. Generous free tier. Strong developer experience. Direct enterprise revenue carries the company while API matures.

Card program regulatory complexity

Use BaaS providers (Marqeta/Lithic) who handle compliance. Hire fintech counsel. Card is Year 2-3, not immediate.

Consumer apps don’t see API value

Item-level data is universally desired. Every fintech has wanted this for years. Enterprise revenue proves the value before API GA.

Privacy/data concerns

Privacy-first positioning. SOC 2 compliance. Users own their data. Opt-in only for anonymized aggregation.
